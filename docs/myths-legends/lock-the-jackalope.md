## The real history

### A jackrabbit, a pair of antlers and a bright idea

The modern American jackalope was born in **Douglas, Wyoming, in the 1930s**. As told in Douglas Herrick's 2003 obituaries, Doug and his younger brother Ralph had learned taxidermy by mail order. After a hunt, they tossed a jackrabbit into their shop, and it came to rest against a pair of deer antlers. Ralph recalled that it "looked like that rabbit had horns on it." Doug is credited with the idea of mounting it that way.[^herrick]

The brothers sold that first mount for **$10** to Roy Ball, who hung it in the Hotel LaBonte in Douglas. It hung there until it was **stolen in 1977**, and the thief has never been caught.[^herrick] Doug Herrick, by the way, served as a **B-17 tail gunner** in World War II.[^herrick]

*Note on the year:* sources disagree about when the first mount was made, with dates from 1932 to 1940, so we just say "the 1930s."[^herrick]

### The hunting license

Douglas leaned into the joke. The town calls itself the "Home of the Jackalope" and has an eight-foot jackalope statue. It also issues novelty **Jackalope Hunting Licenses**, valid only on **June 31** (a date that doesn't exist) between midnight and 2 a.m., and only for hunters with an IQ over 50 but not over 72.[^douglas] Bills to make the jackalope Wyoming's official mythological creature passed the state House several times and died in the Senate each time.[^wiki]

### Old-World ancestors

Horned hares go back long before Wyoming. The Swiss naturalist **Conrad Gessner** described a *Lepus cornutus* (horned hare) in his *Historiae animalium* in the 1550s, and the creature turned up in natural-history books for about two more centuries. German folklore still has its cousins, the Bavarian **wolpertinger** and Thuringia's **rasselbock**.[^wiki]

### A grain of truth: the Shope papillomavirus

In **1933**, Dr. Richard E. Shope of the Rockefeller Institute described a virus that caused horn-like growths on wild cottontail rabbits.[^shope] That virus became the first known DNA tumor virus and an important model for later research on human papillomavirus (HPV).[^javier] Researchers think rabbits with these growths may lie behind some horned-rabbit legends. That's a plausible hypothesis, not established fact.

## The lore *(tall tale, all of it)*

- **Singing and mimicry:** jackalopes copy the voices of cowboys singing around the campfire, usually in tenor.
- **Dangerous when cornered:** peaceable until cornered, then "downright vicious when threatened." Hunters are advised to wear stovepipes on their legs.
- **Scarcity:** they breed only during flashes of lightning, which explains why you rarely see one.

## Lock: the freedom allegory

Lock is a myth, and he knows it. Myths still teach, though, and the traits cowboys and shopkeepers gave this critter make a fair portrait of a free citizen:

| Jackalope trait | What it stands for |
|---|---|
| Horns for defense, never conquest | **2nd Amendment.** A peaceable people who remain able to defend themselves. |
| No census, no catalog, hunting only on June 31 | **4th Amendment.** Secure in "persons, houses, papers, and effects." |
| The mimic who knows a fake voice | **1st Amendment.** Sees through propaganda and answers lies with free speech. |
| Creature of the open range | **Property rights** and **5th Amendment** "just compensation." |
| Famous because of a small town, a chamber of commerce and a state legislature | **10th Amendment.** Local is better. No federal Department of Jackalopes. |
| Two brothers, a mail-order course and a happy accident | **Free enterprise.** Nobody wrote a five-year plan for the jackalope. |

The contrast with 1917 is no accident. The Bolsheviks disarmed the people by decree, created a secret police that published lists of "enemies of the people," and shut down opposition newspapers. (See **[Russia 1917: From Bread to the Cheka](../history/russia-1917-bread-to-cheka.md)**.) Lock keeps his antlers.

## The Jackalope Creed

> I keep my horns for defense, never for conquest.  
> My rights come from my Creator, not from a commissar.  
> I will not be registered, rounded up, or collectivized.  
> I know a borrowed voice when I hear one, and I sing in my own.  
> What I raise is mine to keep, to trade, or to give freely.  
> I keep the peace, and I keep my powder dry.  
> Wide range, small government, free people. That's the West I defend.
