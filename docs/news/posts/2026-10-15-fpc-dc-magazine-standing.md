# FPC asks the full D.C. Circuit to let gun owners into court

**What happened.** FPC filed an opening brief and a petition for initial hearing en banc in *Wehr-Darroca v. District of Columbia*, its challenge to D.C.'s ban on magazines holding more than ten rounds. A district court dismissed the case under D.C. Circuit precedent (*Navegar*, *Seegars* and *Parker*) that requires a heightened showing of a specific threat of prosecution before a gun owner can sue. Under the ban, violators face a felony punishable by up to three years in prison and a $12,500 fine. FPC also argues that its members' cost of buying restricted-capacity replacement magazines gives them standing under the court's recent *Angelo v. District of Columbia* decision.

<!-- more -->

**Why it matters.** FPC says the D.C. Circuit treats Second Amendment cases differently from other constitutional cases. Translation: to challenge a gun ban there, you practically have to volunteer for prosecution first. As FPC President Brandon Combs put it, "The Second Amendment is not a second-class right, and gun owners are not second-class citizens."
