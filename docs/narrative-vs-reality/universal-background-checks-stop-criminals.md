# "Universal background checks stop criminals"

!!! quote "The Narrative"
    "Close the loopholes. Require a background check on every gun sale, and criminals won't be able to get guns."

**Verdict: Mostly not supported.** The Justice Department's own survey of prisoners found that **9 in 10** of those who had a gun during their crime did **not** get it from a store, pawn shop, flea market or gun show. Most got it on the street, from family or friends, by theft, or at the crime scene. When the existing check *does* catch a prohibited buyer who lied on the federal form, the federal government almost never prosecutes. And California's universal-check law, the best-studied test case, showed no measurable effect on gun homicide.

## The record: where armed criminals got their guns

The Bureau of Justice Statistics interviewed a national sample of state and federal prisoners in 2016. About **287,400** prisoners said they had a gun during the crime they were serving time for. Among those who said where it came from:[^bjs]

| Source of the firearm | Share |
|---|---:|
| **Off the street / underground market** | **43.2%** |
| From family or friends (bought, borrowed, gift, or bought for them) | 25.3% |
| Other (found at the crime scene, brought by someone else, etc.) | 17.4% |
| **Retail source, total** | **10.1%** |
| ... gun shop or store | 7.5% |
| ... pawn shop | 1.6% |
| ... **gun show** | **0.8%** |
| ... flea market | 0.4% |
| Theft | 6.4% |

*Prisoners could name more than one source, so shares add to more than 100%.*

**Key points:**

- **89.9%** did not buy or trade for the gun at any retail source. BJS: "Among prisoners who possessed a gun during their offense, 90% did not obtain it from a retail source."[^bjs]
- Only **0.8%** got it at a gun show, the setting the "loophole" debate usually focuses on.[^bjs]
- About **7%** bought it under their own name from a licensed dealer, the channel that already requires a federal check. Of all prisoners in the survey, **fewer than 2%** had a gun from a retail source during their crime.[^bjs]

## The record: denied buyers are rarely prosecuted

Lying on the federal form (ATF Form 4473) to buy a gun is a federal crime. In fiscal year 2017, the Government Accountability Office found:[^gao]

| Step | FY 2017 |
|---|---:|
| Federal NICS background checks | 8,606,286 |
| Denied transactions | 112,090 |
| Referred by ATF to field offices for investigation | 12,710 |
| **Prosecuted by U.S. Attorneys** (as of June 2018) | **12** |

That is about **1 prosecution for every 9,300 denials.** *(Our arithmetic from GAO's table.)* GAO also found that officials from 10 of the 13 states it studied that run their own checks "do not investigate or prosecute NICS denials."[^gao]

## The record: California's universal checks

California has required a background check on nearly all gun sales, including private sales, since **1991**. It also barred people convicted of certain violent misdemeanors from owning guns. Researchers from UC Davis and Johns Hopkins compared California with 32 other states over 1981–2000. They found:[^cbc]

> "The simultaneous implementation of CBC and MVP policies was not associated with a net change in the firearm homicide rate over the ensuing 10 years in California."

The authors themselves suggested reasons: "incomplete and missing records for background checks, incomplete compliance and enforcement, and narrowly constructed prohibitions."[^cbc]

## What's left out, on both sides

To be fair to supporters of universal checks:

- **Some guns flow through private sales.** A quarter of armed prisoners got their gun from family or friends.[^bjs] Some of those transfers could, in principle, have been subject to a check. Many "off the street" guns also started out in a legal sale before being trafficked or stolen.
- **The research isn't all one way.** The same California study notes that stricter **permit-to-purchase** laws, which require a police-issued permit before any sale, have shown benefits in some studies.[^cbc] Other researchers argue that universal checks make gun trafficking harder over time. That evidence is disputed, and the best-studied universal-check law did not show a homicide effect.
- **Prisoner surveys are self-reported.** Inmates may shade the truth about where they got a gun. Still, the 2016 survey had a 70% response rate and is the government's own best source on the question.[^bjs]

What gets left out of the campaign ads is simpler. A check only works on a sale that goes through a check. Street dealers, thieves and straw buyers don't line up for one. And when the system *does* catch a lying felon, the law is almost never enforced.

## Bottom line

Criminals mostly get guns where no background check will ever reach: the street, the burglary, the friend or relative. Expanding checks to every private sale between law-abiding neighbors puts the burden on the people least likely to commit crimes. If the goal is keeping guns from criminals, start by **prosecuting the prohibited people the current system already catches**, and go after theft and trafficking.
