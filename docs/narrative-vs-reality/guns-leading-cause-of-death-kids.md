# "Guns are the leading cause of death for kids"

!!! quote "The Narrative"
    "Guns are now the leading cause of death for American children."

**Verdict: True as the CDC words it, misleading as usually used.** The statistic is real. But "children" in the headline version includes 18- and 19-year-old adults, and most of the deaths are homicides and suicides among older teens, not the accidental shootings of young children that the slogan brings to mind.

## The record

- The **CDC** states that "firearm injuries were the leading cause of death among children and teens ages 1–19" in 2022.[^cdc] Note the age range. It starts at **1** and ends at **19**.
- A 2022 letter in the *New England Journal of Medicine* found that firearm injuries **became** the leading cause of death for ages 1–19 in **2020**, passing motor-vehicle crashes. Before that they had been second.[^nejm]
- Johns Hopkins researchers report that firearms have also been the leading cause of death for ages **1–17** since 2020. They add that, for some individual youth age groups, firearms are "among the leading causes, but not always the top cause."[^jhu23]

So we're not calling the claim false. We're asking what it measures.

## What's left out

### 1. Many of these "kids" are adults

In 2021, CDC data counted **4,752** firearm deaths among Americans aged **0–19**.[^peds] Pew Research's analysis of the same CDC database counted **2,590** firearm deaths among those **under 18** that year.[^pew]

That leaves about **2,160 deaths, or roughly 45% of the 0–19 total, among 18- and 19-year-olds.** They are legal adults who can vote, enlist and sign contracts. *(This is our arithmetic, 4,752 − 2,590, from two published counts of the same 2021 CDC data.)*

### 2. The deaths are concentrated among older teens

- In the 2021 data (ages 0–19), **82.6%** of firearm deaths were among those aged **15–19**.[^peds]
- Among those under 18 in 2021, ages **12–17** accounted for **86%** of gun deaths. Children 11 and under accounted for about 14% (363 deaths), and each of those deaths is a tragedy.[^pew]
- In 2023 (ages 1–17), the counts were 118 deaths (ages 1–4), 116 (5–9), 530 (10–14) and **1,802 (15–17)**.[^jhu23]

### 3. Mostly homicide and suicide, not accidents

Of the 2021 firearm deaths among ages 0–19, **64.3% were homicides, 29.9% suicides, and 3.5% unintentional**.[^peds] Among those under 18, Pew found 60% homicide, 32% suicide and 5% accidents.[^pew]

So the main problems are teen violence and teen suicide. Each one has different causes and needs different answers. A policy aimed at the type of gun or the law-abiding owner doesn't touch either problem directly.

### 4. Infants are excluded

The CDC's "ages 1–19" framing leaves out children under age 1, whose deaths researchers usually analyze separately. That's a standard choice, but it shows again that the headline number depends on which age bracket someone picks.[^cdc][^nejm]

## Bottom line

The claim is accurate for the age range used. But a "kid" in that statistic is usually a 15-to-19-year-old, nearly half of the 0–19 total are 18 or 19, and more than nine in ten of the deaths are homicides or suicides. Grief is real, and so are the numbers. Solutions should follow the numbers, which point to violent crime and teen mental health, not to the rights of law-abiding adults.
