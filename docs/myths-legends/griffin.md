## Description

The griffin is the old one. Classical naturalists gave it the body of a lion and the head and wings of an eagle, and set it to guard a hoard of gold. It does not share. Travelers who came back from the edge of the map said the beast would take a horse apart before it would let a stranger pocket a coin. Coats of arms still carry the picture. A live specimen has been harder to produce.

That is the usual problem with a griffin. The newer problem is what anyone is supposed to hunt it *with*.

## Habitat and sightings

Heraldry, monastery carvings, and the margins of medieval bestiaries. Modern confirmed sightings of a living griffin: none. Modern confirmed sightings of people who are quite sure they have met its natural predator: constant. The predator in question is [The "Assault Weapon"](assault-weapon.md). A mythical beast, the old rule says, requires a mythical weapon.

## The hunting problem

Hunters who want a griffin therefore go looking for an "assault weapon," or, when the flyer is feeling specific, an "assault rifle." This is inconvenient. That creature is itself only ever sighted at buyback tables, in press releases, and in the text of a bill. An ordinary gun owner who goes looking for one in the rack at home, or on the bench at the range, finds a rifle that works like other rifles. The griffin-hunting model has left the area.

On **October 20, 2026**, New York Attorney General Letitia James's office reported a community buyback in Rochester that took in **606** guns, of which **44** were recorded as "assault weapons."[^james] The flyer for that event offered **$600** for an "assault rifle." Payment, the flyer said, was "subject to approval." It did not say what the animal looked like, how it differed from the ordinary rifle or shotgun listed farther down the card, or which feathers had to be present before the table would call it one. Classification, like the gift card, was subject to approval.

So the scorekeeping is real, and the species still is not. Forty-four specimens were logged in one city on one day. None of them has been produced for a field guide that asks what the creature *is*, as distinct from what the table decided to pay. The griffin, somewhere off the edge of the map, remains unbothered. Its appointed hunter is still a rumor with a price tag.

## Field notes

- **Preferred ground:** a folding table, a press release, and a statute that identifies the beast by its accessories.
- **Flight response:** the weapon vanishes when a gun owner asks which feature made this rifle the quarry and the next rifle a deer gun.
- **Bounty:** $600, "subject to approval," definition not included.
- **Recommended season:** any day the flyer is up. The griffin remains at large.

**See also:** [The "Assault Weapon"](assault-weapon.md), the only arm said to be equal to this hunt, and the one creature in the guide that is harder to find than the griffin.
